1. Escaping Saturation Degeneracy: Method Comparison

When presence/absence vectors are saturated within target clades (all 1s within resistant lineages, all 0s elsewhere), presence/absence statistics lose statistical power. The problem shifts from state presence to molecular evolutionary dynamics.

High-Value Public Genomes Across Independent Origins

To break clade saturation, sample single or dual representative genomes from geographically and phylogenetically distant clades:

Actinomycetota (Bacterial Origin 2): Rubrobacter radiotolerans or Kineococcus radiotolerans

Deinococcus-Thermus (Bacterial Origin 1): Truepera radiovictrix (deeply diverged relative to core Deinococcus)

Methanobacteriota (Archaeal Origin): Methanobacterium formicicum or Halobacterium salinarum (extreme DNA repair machinery, independent archaeal origin)

Tardigrada (Eukaryotic Origin 1): Ramazzottius varieornatus or Hypsibius exemplaris

Rotifera (Eukaryotic Origin 2): Adineta vaga or Didymodactylos carnosus

Nematoda (Eukaryotic Origin 3): Panagrolaimus superbus (desiccation/radiation resistant)

Minimum Sample Size Calculation

For phylogenetic binary association tests (e.g., Firth-corrected Firth-logistic regression or Pagel's 1994 discrete correlation model), statistical power requires a minimum of N≥4 to 5 independent phenotypic transitions.

Sampling 4 distinct bacterial origins + 2 distinct eukaryotic origins yields 6 independent transitions, providing sufficient degrees of freedom to avoid null degeneracy.

If adding data is restricted, reframe the exact finding: "Conserved Co-Retention Across Deep Metazoan-Bacterial Divides."
