\section{Introduction (skeleton)}
Extremotolerant organisms have evolved radiation resistance independently at least three times: in the bacterial family Deinococcaceae, in tardigrades, and in bdelloid rotifers. Whether these origins converged on the same molecular machinery is unknown. We test whether orthogroups are convergently retained across resistant lineages while absent from radiation-sensitive controls.

\section{Background: The Biology of Extreme Radiation Resistance}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Ionizing radiation as a selective force}
Ionizing radiation damages cells through two routes. Direct deposition of
energy in DNA produces double-strand breaks (DSBs), the most lethal common
lesion; a single unrepaired DSB can be sufficient to kill a dividing cell.
Indirect damage is mediated by water radiolysis, which generates hydroxyl
radicals, superoxide, and hydrogen peroxide, and these reactive oxygen
species (ROS) oxidize proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids far from the site
of the original photon absorption. For desiccation-tolerant organisms the
indirect route dominates: at low water activity the primary lesion burden
shifts toward oxidative protein carbonylation, and survival correlates more
strongly with proteome protection than with DNA repair capacity alone.

\subsection{The canonical model: \emph{Deinococcus radiodurans}}
\emph{D. radiodurans} R1 survives acute gamma doses above 10~kGy without
loss of viability, roughly three orders of magnitude beyond the lethal dose
for \emph{Escherichia coli}. Three decades of work have established that its
resistance is not attributable to unusual target hardening of DNA. Instead,
a layered defense acts on the proteome and the metabolome: (i)~manganese
accumulation, with intracellular Mn(II) concentrations reaching millimolar
levels and Mn/Fe ratios far above those of sensitive bacteria; (ii)~Mn(II)
complexes with orthophosphate and small metabolites (peptides, nucleosides)
that scavenge superoxide and hydroxyl radicals non-enzymatically; (iii)~an
unusually deep enzymatic antioxidant battery, including multiple catalases,
superoxide dismutases, and peroxiredoxins of the AhpC/TSA family; and
(iv)~an efficient homologous-recombination machinery operating on a
multipartite, polyploid genome that reassembles shattered chromosomes from
overlapping fragments through extended synthesis-dependent strand
annealing. The resulting picture is that of a cell whose DNA is damaged at
the same rate as any other cell's but whose proteins remain functional
throughout irradiation, permitting rapid repair once conditions allow.

\subsection{Convergent resistance in other clades}
Extreme resistance has evolved independently in several lineages, and each
case is an independent natural experiment. Within bacteria, the
Deinococcaceae contain multiple resistant species (\emph{D. radiodurans},
\emph{D. geothermalis}, \emph{D. deserti}), and the family is treated in
this study as a single origin because of shared ancestry. Among animals,
tardigrades such as \emph{Ramazzottius varieornatus} tolerate kilogray
doses in the desiccated state, and at least one lineage-specific protein,
Dsup, binds nucleosomes and reduces DNA damage in heterologous systems,
including human cells. Bdelloid rotifers such as \emph{Adineta vaga}
combine desiccation tolerance with radiation resistance and carry heavily
reorganized genomes with signatures of horizontal gene transfer, including
genes of bacterial and fungal origin implicated in stress response. These
three origins - Deinococcaceae, Tardigrada, and Bdelloidea - are separated
by more than a billion years of evolution, so any molecular feature shared
across them is a candidate convergence on a common solution rather than a
shared inheritance.

\subsection{Why oxidative defense is the leading axis}
Across resistant taxa the recurring observation is elevated antioxidant
capacity: peroxiredoxins, glutaredoxin and thioredoxin systems, flavin
reductases that recycle redox cofactors, and metal-homeostasis systems
that limit iron-catalyzed Fenton chemistry. Peroxiredoxins of the
AhpC/TSA family are peroxide-scavenging enzymes with exceptionally high
catalytic rates; their up-regulation is a common response to irradiation
in bacteria, and their abundance correlates with desiccation tolerance in
tardigrades and rotifers. If radiation resistance across kingdoms shares a
single dominant mechanistic axis, comparative genomics should recover
orthogroups in this pathway as convergently retained or expanded in
resistant lineages while absent from sensitive relatives - the hypothesis
tested here.

\subsection{The specificity problem}
A central confound is that desiccation, oxidation, heat, and radiation
stresses are correlated in nature. Organisms that survive drying
necessarily solve the oxidative-damage problem, and radiation resistance
may in part be a byproduct of desiccation adaptation. Any genomic
enrichment found in resistant species could therefore reflect general
stress adaptation rather than radiation-specific selection. This study
addresses the confound directly with a pre-registered, stress-matched
control panel (Section~\ref{sec:stresspanel}): stress-tolerant but
radiation-sensitive species are subjected to the identical pipeline, and
candidate orthogroups must be absent from that panel to retain a
radiation-specific interpretation.

\subsection{Known mechanisms of ionizing-radiation resistance}
Decades of work on \emph{Deinococcus radiodurans} established that extreme
resistance is not conferred by a single pathway. Three mechanistic layers are
recognized. First, an exceptionally clean antioxidant defense: high cellular
Mn$^{2+}$/Fe$^{2+}$ ratios, manganese-phosphate metabolite complexes, and
small-molecule antioxidants protect proteins --- and especially the DNA-repair
enzymes themselves --- from oxidative inactivation, so that an otherwise
conventional repair toolkit keeps working at doses that disable it in
sensitive cells. Second, efficient and redundant DNA-repair capacity:
extended synthesis-dependent strand annealing, RecA-dependent homologous
recombination, and non-homologous end-joining reassemble chromosomes
shattered into hundreds of fragments. Third, genome organization and
proteome protection: nucleoid condensation, redundant genome copies, and
chaperone/protease systems limit and clear damaged protein. Tardigrades add
distinct intrinsically disordered protective proteins (CAHS, SAHS, and the
DNA-shielding Dsup), demonstrating that more than one molecular route to
resistance exists; bdelloid rotifers combine desiccation-linked antioxidant
defense with horizontal-gene-transfer-enriched damage-response repertoires.
A comparative screen across independent origins therefore asks not whether a
single known factor is shared --- none is --- but which \emph{modules} were
independently retained, which is the question the locked gates operationalize.

\subsection{Why convergence is the informative signal}
Single-organism studies cannot separate general stress biology from
radiation-specific adaptation, and single-lineage comparative genomics cannot
separate ancestry from adaptation. Convergent retention across independent
extremotolerant origins --- here a bacterial family and a metazoan class ---
restricts attention to modules that selection kept rebuilding or re-keeping
under the same pressure, while the stress-matched radiation-sensitive control
panel removes modules that merely mark hardy organisms in general. This
design logic follows the comparative strategy that identified convergent
desiccation machinery, applied here to the radiation axis with pre-registered
gates so that every retained or discarded candidate carries a stated reason.

\subsection{Formal framework}
Each orthogroup $g$ is a partition of the panel proteomes into member sets; for species $i$ let $n_{gi} \ge 0$ be the copy number and $x_{gi} = \mathbf{1}[n_{gi} > 0]$ the presence indicator. The resistant and sensitive label vectors $y \in \{0,1\}^{11}$ are fixed by the panel definition. The presence/absence arm applies a one-sided Fisher exact test to the $2\times2$ table of $x_g$ against $y$; under complete separation (all resistant present, all sensitive absent) the minimum achievable value is
\begin{equation}
p_{\min} \;=\; \frac{1}{\binom{11}{5}} \;=\; 2.16\times10^{-3},
\end{equation}
which motivated replacing the BH-FDR gate by permutation calibration (pre-outcome amendment). The empirical $p$-value for orthogroup $g$ over $B=1000$ label permutations $\pi_b$ is
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_g \;=\; \frac{1 + \sum_{b=1}^{B} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ T(x_g, \pi_b(y)) \ge T(x_g, y) \right]}{1 + B},
\end{equation}
with statistic $T$ the resistant-direction enrichment score; the empirical-$p$ estimator floor is $1/(1+B) = 1/1001 \approx 10^{-3}$; the observed $\hat p = 0.013$ ($13/1001$) sits 13 permutation counts above that floor and is a genuine count-based estimate, not a floor-limited value. The copy-number arm uses a one-sided Mann-Whitney statistic
\begin{equation}
U_g \;=\; \sum_{i:\, y_i = 1} \sum_{j:\, y_j = 0} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ n_{gi} > n_{gj} \right] + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[ n_{gi} = n_{gj} \right]
\end{equation}
on zero-filled copy numbers. The convergence gate additionally requires replication across independent origins:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k \in \{\text{Deino},\, \text{Tardi},\, \text{Bdello}\}} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ \max_{i \in k} x_{gi} = 1 \right] \;\ge\; 2.
\end{equation}

For the specificity layer, each v1 candidate was re-clustered with four stress-tolerant but radiation-sensitive controls and re-tested identically; a candidate keeps the label radiation-specific only if
\begin{equation}
\left| \{ c \in \text{stress controls} : x_{gc} = 1 \} \right| \;\le\; 1,
\end{equation}
otherwise it is re-labeled broad-stress adaptation. Domain-level confirmation searches each candidate's full Pfam architecture $\mathcal{D}_g = \{d_1, \dots, d_m\}$ against the five confirmation proteomes with family HMMs, accepting a proteome as supporting iff
\begin{equation}
\exists\, \text{protein } q : \bigwedge_{d \in \mathcal{D}_g} \left[ E(q, d) \le 10^{-5} \right],
\end{equation}
and confirming the candidate iff at least two of five proteomes support it. The phylogenetic layer (H4--H4c) maps presence onto a fixed cladogram $\tau$ and counts minimum gains by Fitch reconstruction,
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{ind}}(g) \;=\; \min_{\text{ASR}} \; \sum_{e \in \tau} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ 0 \to 1 \text{ on } e,\; e \text{ subtends a resistant leaf} \right],
\end{equation}
against a null of $10^{4}$ within-strata presence shuffles $\sigma_r$ preserving stratum counts,
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{lower}}(g) \;=\; \frac{1 + \sum_{r} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ N_{\text{ind}}^{\sigma_r} \le N_{\text{ind}}(g) \right]}{1 + 10^{4}},
\end{equation}
which proved degenerate on saturated strata (zero null variance), yielding the identifiability boundary reported in Section 3.4.

\subsection{Phylogenetically controlled convergence test (amendments H4--H4c, 2026-09-26)}
The strongest remaining objection to the v2 labels is phylogenetic confounding: the three \emph{Deinococcus} proteomes (like the two \emph{Thermus} controls) are not independent replicates, so a label permutation that treats species as exchangeable only partially randomizes family-level structure. We therefore implemented a phylogenetically controlled convergence test, locked before computation (amendment H4): for each radiation-specific candidate, presence/absence was mapped onto a fixed 15-species cladogram (topology from NCBI taxonomy, unit branch lengths), and the minimum number of independent gains into resistant-subtending branches was computed by Fitch-parsimony ancestral-state reconstruction (the minimum-gain count is method-independent; a PastML maximum-likelihood cross-check is queued). The null was 10{,}000 structure-preserving permutations that shuffle presence assignments only within exchangeable strata (Deinococcaceae, Thermus, other controls), never across.
Formally, let $x_g \in \{0,1\}^{|\mathrm{leaves}(\tau)|}$ be the presence vector of candidate $g$ on the fixed cladogram $\tau$. The Fitch bottom-up pass assigns each internal node $v$ the intersection of its children's state sets when non-empty, else their union, and the minimum-gain statistic is the number of union events,
\begin{equation}
G(x_g;\tau) \;=\; \sum_{v \in \mathrm{int}(\tau)} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ S_v = S_{v_1} \cup S_{v_2} \right],
\end{equation}
where $S_v$ is the Fitch state set of node $v$ with children $v_1, v_2$, and a union event implies exactly one gain on the branch subtending the child absent the state. The structure-preserving null draws permutations $\pi_b$ that shuffle presence labels only within exchangeable strata $\mathcal{S} \in \{\text{Deinococcaceae}, \text{Thermus}, \text{other controls}\}$, and the corrected (lower-tail, amendment H4c) empirical convergence $p$-value is
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}^{\,\mathrm{conv}}_g \;=\; \frac{1 + \sum_{b=1}^{B} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ G(\pi_b(x_g);\tau) \le G(x_g;\tau) \right]}{1 + B},
\end{equation}
with $B = 10{,}000$; the lower tail is the convergence-consistent direction because scattered presence forces \emph{more} gains, so unusually \emph{few} gains reject the structured null.

\textbf{The test returned a structural null}: for all ten candidates the null distribution has zero variance (observed 2 gains, null median 2, $p=1.0$). The cause is identifiability, not biology: every candidate is present in all three Deinococcaceae, and the Tardigrada and Bdelloidea origins are singletons whose labels cannot be permuted, so no within-strata shuffle can move the signal. Resistance labels are perfectly confounded with tree topology on this panel.
